# Improving email deliverability with DNS records

Mail eXchangers (Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook) check the validity of
incoming messages. As part of the validation, the Mail eXchanger
queries DNS records of the sender and evaluates the sender's
**reputation**. The reputation affects whether a message lands in
the inbox, the spam folder, or gets blocked entirely before it
reaches the receiver.

## The reputation scale

Four levels:

- **Very low** — blocked completely. Yahoo's
  `421 4.7.0 [TS01] Messages temporarily deferred due to user
  complaints` is a typical bounce. High-volume senders should
  consider a **dedicated IP** with a warm-up mechanism — a way of
  saying "I own a unique IP because I'm confident Mail eXchangers
  won't block me."
- **Low** — emails marked as spam due to low authenticity or
  generic content. Bad: "Hi Willy, here's a body lotion ad" sent
  to someone interested in computer science.
- **Standard** — messages reach the inbox, though some Mail
  eXchangers (Gmail) may move them to the **Promotions** tab.
- **Best** — messages reach the inbox as intended.

## DNS record baseline

| Type  | Purpose                          | Example                                                       |
| ----- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A     | IP addresses                     | `virtual-machine.meirg.co.il` → `123.123.123.123`             |
| CNAME | Canonical name for another name  | `meirg.co.il` → `meirg-website.pages.dev` (Cloudflare Pages)  |
| TXT   | Domain validation/authenticity   | `meirg.co.il` → `"google-site-verification=2NOO....XgotefAU"` |
| MX    | Mail eXchangers receiving emails | (covered later)                                               |

## The four standards (per Google, in this order)

1. **SPF**
2. **DKIM**
3. **DMARC**
4. (Optional) **BIMI**

Plus: **a dedicated IP**.

### SPF — Sender Policy Framework

Remember with: **S** for "Sending."

A TXT record listing the services authorized to send email on
behalf of your domain. Example combining Google Workspace and
SendGrid:

```
# Google Workspace
Type: TXT
Name: meirg.co.il
Value: v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all

# SendGrid
Type: TXT
Name: em123.meirg.co.il
Value: u12345678.wl123.sendgrid.net
```

SendGrid's CNAME-style entry resolves to a real SPF expression
when looked up — eventually you'll see `v=spf1 ip4:122.122.122.122 -all`.
That `ip4:` form is a **dedicated IP**.

When you need multiple ESPs in the same SPF record on the root
domain, combine them:

```
# Google Workspace + Mailgun
Type: TXT
Name: meirg.co.il
Value: v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com include:mailgun.org ~all
```

> **Important**: send emails from the same address you've
> authenticated in your ESP. Don't send from `no-reply@meirg.com`
> when your authenticated domain is `meirg.co.il`.

### DKIM — DomainKeys Identified Mail

Remember with: **K** for "Keys."

Think of DKIM as the HTTPS of emails. It adds an encrypted
signature to outgoing message headers. Mail servers decrypt it
to verify the message wasn't changed in transit.

```
Type: TXT
Name: google._domainkey.meirg.co.il
Value: v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=Ultra-Long-Public-Key-392-chars
```

The subdomain `google._domainkey` was generated by Google for my
domain. Other ESPs use their own selectors — SendGrid uses
`s1._domainkey`. The `*._domainkey` prefix is what the Mail
eXchanger looks up. The selector (`google`, `s1`) lets one domain
have multiple DKIM signatures for different ESPs.

### DMARC — Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting & Conformance

Remember with: **DM** for "Domain Monitoring," or **D-MARC** like
"the manager."

DMARC is the **enforcement layer** over SPF and DKIM. Three
things it does:

- Sends a **daily report** to a predefined mailbox with
  pass/fail counts for SPF and DKIM.
- Honors a **policy** — `p=reject` blocks unauthenticated mail,
  `p=none` is monitor-only.
- Authenticates the sender's identity (combination of SPF + DKIM
  + DMARC).

```
Type: TXT
Name: _dmarc.meirg.co.il
Value: v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:meir+dmarc_agg@meirg.co.il, mailto:dmarc_agg@vali.email
```

Why `p=none`? I'm still not 100% sure my outgoing messages pass
SPF + DKIM. `p=reject` would block them entirely. Start with
`p=none` for monitoring; flip to `p=reject` once the daily
reports show 100% pass rate.

The `rua` key is where DMARC reports go. I added `+dmarc_agg`
as a Gmail filter tag, plus **ValiMail** (`dmarc_agg@vali.email`)
as a free DMARC analytics service partnered with Microsoft,
Google, and SendGrid.

### BIMI — Brand Indicators for Message Identification

Remember with: **B** for "Bragging."

Requires DMARC at `p=reject, pct=100` (or `p=quarantine`),
trademark registration, and a Verified Mark Certificate (VMC)
from DigiCert or Entrust. BIMI is a nice-to-have, not yet widely
enforced. It'll likely be easier to qualify today than in a few
years.

## MX — Mail eXchanger

Remember with: Gmail is a Mail eXchanger; Google Workspace is an
ESP.

MX records define which mail exchangers receive email on your
behalf. Gmail acts as both a Mail eXchanger and as a sending
service (when you compose `@gmail.com` mail Gmail handles SPF +
DKIM + DMARC for you behind the scenes).

## Strengthen your knowledge

### Name all the standards in one line each

1. **SPF** — TXT record listing allowed sending sources
   (domains/IPs).
2. **DKIM** — TXT/CNAME record with a public key generated by
   the ESP. The HTTPS of emails.
3. **DMARC** — TXT record defining the policy and the reporting
   address.
4. **BIMI** — TXT record with an SVG logo URL and the VMC URL.

### Reading a DMARC expression

Gmail's:

```
v=DMARC1; p=none; sp=quarantine; rua=mailto:mailauth-reports@google.com
```

- `p=none` — all emails reach the receiver regardless of SPF +
  DKIM.
- `sp=quarantine` — overrides `p` for **subdomains** (e.g.
  `subdomain.gmail.com`) — unsigned messages from subdomains hit
  spam.
- `rua=mailto` — Google handles their reports internally
  (`@google.com`).

Yahoo's:

```
v=DMARC1; p=reject; pct=100; rua=mailto:d@rua.agari.com; mailto:d@ruf.agari.com;
```

- `p=reject` — unauthenticated messages are rejected.
- `pct=100` — apply that policy to 100% of failing messages.

### Bonus — Gmail "Show Original"

In Gmail, click the ellipsis on a message → **Show Original**.
You'll see the raw message including the **DMARC: PASS** /
**FAIL** header. Checking DMARC alone is enough — it relies on
both SPF and DKIM.

## Final words

Once you understand how each component works, mailing DNS
records aren't intimidating. Set up SPF first, then DKIM, then
DMARC at `p=none` to monitor, then move to `p=reject` once you
trust the numbers.
